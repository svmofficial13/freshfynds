In mid-November my terraces are as colourful as they get all year. All four of my bougainvilleas are in colour right now. But they are not equal. The white one is covered from end to end. My two oldest plants, the magenta and the yellow, only have colour at the tips of bare, tangled branches.

So I compared the four of them properly. This is what I found, what I can't prove yet, and what I'm changing.

A quick note first: the bright "flowers" on a bougainvillea are not petals. They are bracts, a kind of modified leaf. The real flower is the small white tube in the middle of each cluster.

## My four plants

| Plant | Age | Direct sun | Flowering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magenta | about 10 years | 6–7 hours | 2–3 flushes a year |
| Yellow | 8–9 years | 6–7 hours | 2–3 flushes a year |
| White | 7–8 years | 9+ hours (moved up 2–3 months ago) | 2–3 flushes a year, the heaviest |
| Thimma (variegated, two-colour) | 2 years | 6–7 hours | lighter |

All four get the same care, and it isn't much:

- Plain garden soil since the day they were potted. Never repotted.
- Never pruned.
- Water once a day in summer, 2–3 times a week in winter.
- Food: urea once, about a year ago, and vermicompost in early November. Nothing else.

The white one sits on the top floor in open sun. The other three are one level down, with a shed behind them that cuts their sun.

## Myth vs reality

**Myth:** A healthy bougainvillea should flower all year round.

**Reality:** Bougainvillea flowers in flushes. The colour forms on new growth, so after each flush the plant has to grow fresh shoots before it can colour again. In the North Indian plains it puts on a big show in the hot months and again around October–November, and slows down in the cold. Two or three flushes a year, like mine, is normal.

**Myth:** More water and more fertiliser give more flowers.

**Reality:** The opposite. Too much fertiliser pushes leaves instead of colour, and bougainvillea dislikes soil that stays wet. ICAR's advice for potted plants is to cut watering before a flush and keep it steady during flowering, or the bracts drop early.

## Why the white one wins (and what I can't prove)

![White bougainvillea covered in new lime-green bract clusters](/images/gardening/bougainvillea-white-new-bracts.jpg)

*The white one on 12 November: new bract clusters on almost every shoot. White bracts open lime-green and turn white as they mature.*

Three things are different about it, and I can't separate them:

1. **Sun.** It gets 9+ hours, the others 6–7. Bougainvillea needs strong light to flower. In shade it just makes leaves.
2. **New growth.** Look at the photos. The white one is full of young, leafy shoots. The magenta and yellow are mostly old, bare wood, and old bare wood doesn't flower.
3. **Variety.** Some white varieties are simply very free-flowering. I don't know the name of mine, so I can't confirm this.

![Ten-year-old magenta bougainvillea with colour only at the branch tips and bare wood inside](/images/gardening/bougainvillea-magenta-bare-wood.jpg)

*The 10-year-old magenta: colour at the tips, a tangle of bare twigs inside. That's ten years without pruning.*

One honest detail: the white one flowered well even before I moved it into more sun. So sun is not the whole story. The top floor is also more open and breezy. I found no source saying airflow itself makes bougainvillea flower, so I'm not claiming that.

## The two-colour plant with striped leaves

The plant below is 'Thimma'. It has yellow-and-green leaves and gives both magenta and white bracts. Thimma is a natural sport of the well-known 'Mary Palmer', and it can produce pink, white and mixed pink-white bracts on the same plant.

![Thimma bougainvillea with yellow-and-green striped leaves and pink bracts in a terracotta pot](/images/gardening/bougainvillea-thimma-november.jpg)

*Thimma on 12 November: striped leaves, pink bracts.*

It flowers lighter than the others, and that's not surprising: it's only two years old, and it's one of the plants that gets less sun.

## What I'm changing, season by season

One rule from my lemon experiment applies here too: change one thing at a time and record it, so I know what actually worked.

**Now (November to February):**

- Remove only dead wood. A scratch test tells you: if there's no green under the bark, the twig is dead.
- Water less. Only when the top few centimetres of soil are dry, not on a fixed 2–3 days a week.
- No urea.
- No hard pruning and no repotting in the cold.

**Spring (late February–March, when new leaves start):**

- Check the roots of the old plants. Bougainvillea has delicate roots and doesn't mind a snug pot, so I won't repot just because it's been ten years. If the pot is solid roots, I'll repot. If not, I'll replace the top layer of old soil with a fresh mix.
- A balanced feed. ICAR suggests a balanced NPK, for example 1:1:1. I'll check the dose on the pack and against an official source before using it.

**After the summer flush (end of May–June):**

- The main pruning. ICAR's advice for North India: cut side shoots and old shoots back to two or three buds from the main stems, once flowering is over. The new growth that follows carries the next flush.
- After that, light pinching of shoot tips to keep the plants bushy.

## What I'll report back

I'll update this page with photos from the same angles:

- Spring growth on all four.
- The summer flush, before pruning.
- The regrowth after pruning.
- The October–November flush next year.

If the magenta and yellow catch up with the white after pruning, the old bare wood was the main problem. If the white still wins by a lot, it's the sun or the variety. Either way, I'll tell you honestly.
